OUTLAST VR - BETA 1.0 (final, built as Test 40)
================================================

What changed since the first Beta 1.0 build (Test 39)
------------------------------------------------------
Your feedback on it, and what this build does:

1. "The camcorder is in the wrong position in the hand / not natural like the game's own animation"
   - I recorded the game's own camcorder animation frame by frame (Miles raising it, with the game's
     arms) and copied it: from your eyes you now see what the game shows - the flip-out screen face on,
     the camcorder's side with its dial and ports, Miles's fingers over its top. It sits where your hand
     is; turning the controller no longer twists it (both earlier builds took its angle from the
     controller, which is what looked wrong). Checked high, low and with the controller turned.
2. "Motion controls for climbing (with a setting for the old one)"
   - Climbing with your hands, on by default (VR SETTINGS / in-game menu: "Climb with your hands"; off =
     A climbs as before):
     - A ledge: put both hands on the edge, squeeze both grips (Miles's hands hold the edge where you put
       them), pull down - Miles steps in to the edge if he is a little away from it (your log showed two
       pulls that only made him hop: he was too far for the game), stops, and climbs up, vaults or jumps
       for it, whatever the game has for that ledge. With the camcorder in your right hand, the left hand
       alone does it.
     - Hanging from a ledge: hold on with both grips (his hands on the edge), pull down to climb up, let
       go with both hands to drop.
     - A ladder: walk into it, grab a rung with one hand and pull it down - Miles climbs until your hand
       is back at the rung; grab the next with the other hand. Hand over hand to the top.
     - New how-to labels: CLIMB at an edge, PULL YOURSELF UP while holding on.
3. "When jumping you can move your arms and hands with the native hand animations"
   - Jumps, falls, landings, vaults and crouches keep your arms (the game used to take them for every one
     of these); the fingers play the game's own animation. While climbing, a hand that holds on plays
     Miles's own climbing arm (on the edge, on the rung) and a free hand stays yours.
4. "Opening and pushing doors can be buggy and janky - needs more physics"
   - Found: the door chased a target at a fixed speed - it lagged a fast hand, stopped dead and could jump
     when a hand started past the panel's edge. Now a door you touch is a heavy hinged body: a slow push
     creeps it open, a shove swings it on a little and it slows by itself, your hand stops at the door's
     surface instead of passing through, a hand on the handle pulls through a spring (let go mid-swing
     and it carries on), it bounces softly off its open stop, latches when it swings shut and stops
     against Miles. Anything the game does with a door itself (X, enemies, scripts) still works.

Tested by me (this PC, SteamVR's test headset, scripted hands and buttons)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Camcorder: the game's raise animation captured with its own arms and matched; taken from the hip, held
  high / low / with the controller turned, raised to the eye (camera view), lowered, let go (back on the hip).
- Jump: "arms yours" the whole jump, no arm hand-over.
- Doors: slow push (0.20 -> 0.47), shove (0.16 -> 0.60, swung on at 150 deg/s and stopped in under half a
  second), handle grab and pull (0.51 -> 0.32 through the spring), locked door. The view centre matches
  Miles's camera bone exactly, so held hands line up with handles.
- Ladder: walked into it (the game's own enter move), climbed it hand over hand with scripted grips
  (35 cm pulls = 33 cm climbed), the game's exit on top ran.
- Ledge gesture: on a test edge, both hands took hold, the pull sent A (no real ledge there, so the game
  jumped - the mod saw that and went back to normal). No real ledge was reachable from your save point,
  so the real climb-up / hang / drop runs are NOT tested on a real ledge yet.
- Launcher: the new setting and the CLIMB card render; self-test passes.
- Not tested: real ledges (climb up, hang, drop), your real Quest hands, later levels.

Test 41 (same Beta 1.0 package): "climbing auto-goes into the animation; should feel like Boneworks"
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Your log: walking up to a ladder mounted it by itself (and stepped you off at the top/bottom by
  itself) - canned animations moving your view while your hands did nothing.
- Now your pull drives every climb: the game's climb animations (ledges, walls, getting on a ladder,
  over the top) play only as fast as your holding hands pull down - stop and Miles hangs there, pull
  harder and he goes faster, let go and the game finishes the move. One ~45 cm pull carries a move.
- Ladders: walking into one stops just short of it (the game would take you from ~90 cm); reach out,
  squeeze a rung and pull - Miles steps on as you pull; hand over hand up; pull yourself over the top.
- Tested (test headset, scripted hands): walk-in held at 98 uu; rung taken, mount held still for 3 s
  then followed the pull; hand over hand up; the top exit followed one slow 45 cm pull (177 uu of
  rise) and finished when the hands let go. Ledge climbs use the same pacing (no real ledge reachable
  from your save to test it on).

Test 42 (same Beta 1.0 package): "the arms deform when moving around a lot or crouching"
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Found (measured on the test headset): the bones that shape Miles's sleeves and forearm skin (the
  twist helpers) copied the GAME's own arm twist every frame - and the game swings his arms as he
  walks, runs and crouches: 12-19 degrees of twist walking or sprinting, up to 31 degrees crouching or
  crouch-walking (under 5 standing still). On arms that follow your hands that wrung the wrists and
  sleeves. They now keep the shape from Miles's calm pose and follow your arm only (close-up captures
  crouch-walking: the twisted crease at the left wrist is gone).
- Also: the elbow now bends only like a hinge (the upper arm used to roll as the arm moved far from
  its rest pose, so the elbow bent sideways); a wrist roll is shared between the forearm and the wrist
  instead of all twisting from the elbow; the collarbone lifts a little when you reach high or far.

Test 43 (same Beta 1.0 package): the user's report on Test42
------------------------------------------------------------
"The camcorder pulling it out forward looks wrong ... it still does not go in front of my face and you
don't have much free movement with it. Reverse engineer the climbing to make it more like a native VR
game but still have the old option too. The first jumpscare at the door does not happen when I open it
with motion controls. Add a surprise motion controls feature. The storm is way louder than the last build."
Choices: free-hold camcorder (live screen); cover your mouth ("any moment he is breathing heavy and in
lockers etc.").
- Door scares: the game's own door "open" (AOLDoor 0xD4A770) fires TriggerEvent(0) when the door was
  shut (DoorState 0), sets DoorState 1 and fires TriggerEvent(1) - the outputs of the door's
  OLSeqEvent_Door Kismet events the level scripts' scares listen to. The hand physics never did. Now a door
  the hands open from shut does exactly that, and one they swing shut fires TriggerEvent(2), state 0.
  Verified: X on a shut door logs events 0, 1 (instigator Miles); the hands' pull logs the same, "fired by
  the hands". Also: while Miles opens a door the game's way (X), the hands leave every door near him
  alone until 2 s after (a hand in the swing had pushed a door X opened back to 57%).
- Climbing (ClimbMode=2, physical): a grip on a marked edge or a ladder rung fixes that hand in the world;
  Miles's body (physics off) moves 1:1 against it through the engine's collision-swept AActor::Move;
  hand over hand; the edge at his chest (eyes 50 uu over the lip) and he hauls himself over (feet to the
  lip); a ladder's top steps him off; let go of everything and he drops with the push of your hands.
  Verified on the scaffold ladder: the whole climb hand over hand, ~48 uu per pull, over the platform
  edge, standing on the scaffold (5 uu drop). A hitch no longer drops him (1.5 s grace).
  ClimbMode=1 is the Test41 guided climb, 0 the A button.
- Camcorder (CamcorderHold=4, free): the controller's own angle, the game's grip as its resting angle -
  the lens where the controller points, the back of the camcorder toward you, the flip screen open on its
  left facing you. Its flip screen is live: a quad over the model's screen showing the part of the frame
  the lens points at, in night vision; static when the lens points off the picture. Raise to the eye and
  lower: verified.
- Cover your mouth: Miles's breath sounds are Wwise events (VO_MILES_RUN_BREATH, the STRESS_BREATH set,
  BRE_DAN, the pain breaths...); they are paused (Wwise ExecuteActionOnEvent, 220 ms fade) while a free
  hand is within 11 cm of the mouth, and resumed when it leaves; a gasp (VO_BRE_HGH_Short) after holding
  2.5 s or more; a heartbeat in the covering hand, faster the longer; at 12 s he gasps by himself.
  Verified: a sprint posts VO_MILES_RUN_BREATH; the left hand over the mouth paused it, 5.2 s later
  resumed + the gasp. Available for 6.5 s after any heavy breath, while a stress breath runs, in a locker.
- Storm: the listener (Wwise SetListenerPosition) follows Miles's camera, one listener only; door occlusion
  works as in the game. Measured (WASAPI loopback, 20 s at the same rainy scaffold spot): Test39 median
  -25.5 dBFS, Test42 -28.2, Test43 -25.7 / -25.5 - no build makes it louder. The in-game Volume is at
  maximum in both of the user's profiles (the one profile byte that differs between the two sessions is not
  a sound option). [AUDIODIAG] lines now name every event the game posts, for the next report.
- (later the same day) "Activate the night vision on free hold ... for players wanting more realism; in
  night vision there are black spots and it displays with a small visual issue."
  * Night vision in your hand ([HANDNV]): a right-stick click while you hold the camcorder switches its
    night vision; the live flip screen then amplifies the low light (gain 9, grain) - your eyes stay in
    the dark - or shows the normal colour picture. Raised to the eye, the game's own night vision is
    clicked to match once (OLHero.CamcorderMode: 0 default, 1 powered NV, 2 NV), then the game's switch is
    followed. Verified: on in the hand, raised -> CamcorderMode 1, NV look at the eye.
  * The black spots ([NVEYES]): with each eye's off-centre lens frustum the night-vision lamp lit only the
    inner part of each eye (left eye's left wall and right eye's right wall black - the eyes disagreed).
    Ruled out: the lamp's aim (set once per frame, outside the eye passes), hand shadows, the light
    rectangles (whole eye). With symmetric eyes both are lit alike, so while the game's night vision is
    up the eyes render symmetric, as the zoomed viewfinder already did. Verified in headset captures.

== Test 44 (2026-10-15): walk around for real ==
"Make sure to add where when you move in real life in VR the body in game moves with you too, I will test
it since it would not be possible for you to test that."
- Before: [BODYFOLLOW] dragged the position baseline once the head passed the body bounds (side 12,
  forward 10, back 8 cm) and slid it under a head that sat still 2.5 s - the drag was thrown away, so the
  view stopped at the bound and Miles stayed where he was.
- Now ([ROOMWALK], [Motion] RoomscaleWalk=1): each drag is handed to Miles's feet. ol_xr keeps a running
  total of it with each pose (pending -> latched with the pose at the frame latch); the player tick
  (NativePlayerMove hook, right after the latch) moves Miles by the latched total through AActor::Move
  (collision swept) - body and view move in the same frame. Blocked by more than 40%: a step up (30 uu up,
  across, back down); still blocked = a wall, the view waits at the bound as before. Only while he is on his
  feet in ordinary play (LocomotionMode 0, Physics Walking, no scripted scene, hands not climbing, head
  position on); a head jump over 3.5 m/s is tracking, not walking. Over 40 uu in one frame is capped.
- Verified on the null headset with "headoff" ramps (2.5 cm steps): head 1 m forward -> Miles 90 cm
  (-3990 -> -4081; the last 10 cm stay a lean, then the settle slides the body under the head); 3 m right
  -> 9 cm then the corridor wall: 36 + 45x5 cm held back, the view at the 12 cm side bound against the wall
  (snapshot: at the wall, not through it); back out left 1 m + 1 m backward -> Miles followed both
  (-6980 -> -7068, -4082 -> -3990). [ANCHOR] view vs camera bone stayed inside the bounds (-12, +8 uu).
- Not testable here: real walking on the Quest (speed, feel, stairs). Log lines to read: [ROOMWALK]
  "you walked X cm in your room - Miles walked Y cm with you (Z held back by walls, N steps up)",
  "paused: <why>" / "on", and [BODYFOLLOW] "... cm of it handed to Miles's feet".

  line no longer fires on every spawn.
